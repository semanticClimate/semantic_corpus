**The following file can by no means be regarded as a paper** - it's just some writing on my own. It is meant to clarify a little bit the agrochemicals'
classification, an important topic to address before plunging into their effects.
Not all of them have the same components and not all of them aim to achieve the same results. Being some of these way more aggressive than others,
they should be chosen with great precaution.

### Main Agrochemical Categories

| Category                 | Purpose / Function  | Common Examples  |
|:---------------------------| :--- | :--- |
| **Herbicides**             | Weed control | Glyphosate, Atrazine, 2,4-D |
| **Insecticides**           | Insect control | Permethrin, Imidacloprid, Chlorpyrifos |
| **Fungicides**             | Fungus control | Mancozeb, Azoxystrobin, Tebuconazole |
| **Acaricides (Miticides)** | Mite control | Abamectin, Spirodiclofen, Fenpyroximate |
| **Nematicides**            | Nematode control | Oxamyl, Fluopyram, Fosthiazate |
| **Rodenticides**           | Rodent control | Warfarin, Brodifacoum, Zinc phosphide |
| **Molluscicides**          | Slug and snail control | Metaldehyde, Iron phosphate |
| **Bactericides**           | Bacteria control | Copper hydroxide, Streptomycin, Oxytetracycline |
| **Fertilizers**            | Plant nutrition | NPK (Nitrogen-Phosphorus-Potassium), Urea, Ammonium nitrate |
| **Growth Regulators**      | Modify plant growth and development | Ethephon, Gibberellic acid, Paclobutrazol |
| **Adjuvants**              | Improve application and efficacy of other agrochemicals | Non-ionic surfactants, Crop oil concentrate, Organosilicones |

When it comes to herbicides, they can either be:
 - Selectiv: they would only remove certain species
or
 - Non-selectiv (as Round-Up): they leave almost no remaining vegetation. 

It is also remarkable the relationship between genetically modified organisms and each sort of chemical. The former are becoming more popular across South America 
(especially in Brasil, Argentina, Paraguay, Uruguay, Colombia, Bolivia and Chile - only for seeds!).

### Relationship with Genetically Modified Organisms (GMOs)

| Agrochemical Type | Relationship with Transgenics (GMOs)                                                                                           |
|:------------------|:-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Herbicides**    | Very strong. Crops are genetically engineered to be herbicide-resistant.                                                       |
| **Insecticides**  | Indirect. Some crops produce an insecticidal protein (e.g., Bt crops), reducing the need for chemical insecticide application. |
| **Fungicides**    | Very little direct relationship in commercial crops.                                                                           |
| **Fertilizers**   | No specific relationship with transgenics; both conventional and transgenic crops require plant nutrients.                     |
